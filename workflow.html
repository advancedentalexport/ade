GUIDE 02 / LABORATORY COMMUNICATION

How a restorative case moves between clinic and laboratory.

A clear prescription and readable records help a laboratory understand the requested restoration. This is a typical workflow for indirect restorations; the exact sequence varies with the case, material, and laboratory.

Typical workflow stages

01

Clinical plan and prescription

The dentist establishes the treatment plan and communicates the requested units, material, shade, and design requirements. Relevant clinical photographs and notes can resolve ambiguity.

CLINIC
02

Impression and bite information

The practice records the prepared site, adjacent teeth, opposing arch, and occlusal relationship using suitable conventional or digital methods. Missing margins or incomplete data may require clarification or a new record.

CLINIC → LAB
03

Case review and design

The laboratory reviews the information and plans the restoration in line with the prescription. Digital cases may involve CAD design; conventional cases may involve physical models and related processes.

LAB
04

Fabrication and finishing

Manufacturing may include milling, sintering, casting, ceramic application, finishing, or polishing, depending on the chosen material and design.

LAB
05

Laboratory review and clinical assessment

Laboratory checks may address the restoration's dimensions, surface, contacts, and prescribed details. After delivery, the dentist assesses clinical fit, occlusion, and suitability before final placement.

LAB → CLINIC

CASE COMMUNICATION

Information typically included with a laboratory case

Requirements differ for single crowns, bridges, implant cases, and larger prostheses.

✓

Restoration type, tooth or unit numbers, and material preference

✓

Complete impression or scan of the relevant region

✓

Opposing arch and appropriate bite relationship

✓

Shade, photographs, and aesthetic references when relevant

✓

Special requirements for margins, contacts, or occlusion

✓

Implant system and connection details for implant cases

Patient data: Clinical files should be transferred through approved, secure channels and handled according to applicable privacy obligations. Avoid publishing identifiable case information in a public repository.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Digital and conventional records

Does an intraoral scan replace the laboratory prescription?

No. A scan records surface anatomy, but it does not fully communicate the material choice, design requirements, shade, or treatment plan.

Can a laboratory work from a conventional impression?

Yes. Conventional impressions remain part of many workflows. Their usefulness depends on correct material handling, clear anatomy, and suitable records.

What happens if a scan or impression is incomplete?

The laboratory may need to ask for clarification, additional photographs, a corrected scan, or a new impression before continuing with the work.
