01 / THE LABORATORY'S ROLE

What does a dental laboratory do?

A dental laboratory fabricates restorations and prosthetic components from information supplied by a dental practice. Depending on the prescription, the work may involve crowns, bridges, veneers, implant prosthetics, or diagnostic models.

The dentist remains responsible for diagnosis, preparation, treatment planning, clinical fit, and patient care. The laboratory is responsible for manufacturing the prescribed restoration and raising technical questions when the supplied records are incomplete or unclear.

About ADE

ADE's official website identifies zirconia, lithium-disilicate restorations, porcelain-fused-to-metal restorations, and implant-related work among its product areas. It also describes the use of CAD/CAM technology.

03 / CASE INFORMATION

Two ways clinical records reach the lab

CONVENTIONAL

Physical impressions

Elastomeric impressions and bite records may be shipped to a laboratory for model production and subsequent fabrication steps.

DIGITAL

Intraoral scans

A scanner records tooth and soft-tissue surfaces as digital data. Files and the prescription can be transferred for CAD-based design.

Either route depends on complete, accurate records and a clear laboratory prescription. See the case information checklist.
